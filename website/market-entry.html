What we look at

Four questions, in order.

1

Should you enter?

  • Are people already paying for this, or would you be creating the demand?
  • What can you realistically earn in year one in this city? A national figure tells you little: Johannesburg is not Kimberley.
  • Is there an incumbent, a crowd of small players, or nobody yet?
2

Can you win?

  • What do you have that the incumbent couldn't copy within a year? Being cheaper or faster doesn't count on its own.
  • How will they respond: ignore you, copy you, cut prices or buy you?
3

Can you afford it?

  • What is left from each order after payment costs, refunds, fraud, incentives and delivery?
  • How much money do you need before the market pays for itself, and can you last through a price war?
4

How will you run it?

  • Supply first, then demand. How you get the first 50 to 100 orders, and how many come back.
  • Which numbers to watch each week, and who you hire first.
When we'd say no

Five reasons not to enter.

If any of these is true, we'd advise against launching, at least in the form planned.

  • You'd have to create the demand from scratch.
  • A well-funded incumbent already has the drivers, sellers or customers locked in, and you have nothing they can't copy.
  • The rules are hostile or unclear. Unclear usually means not allowed yet.
  • Realistic year-one revenue is under US$100,000.
  • Nobody has spoken to real customers. Friends and surveys don't count.

Background

Where the method comes from.

From inside three kinds of entry in South Africa: launching a city before the competition arrived, launching a new product nationally, and holding a city while a funded rival came in. Each one showed what to check before the money goes out.

FAQ

Common questions.

Which markets?

South Africa and Zimbabwe, where we've worked on the ground, and the countries around them. For anywhere else, we'll tell you honestly whether we're the right fit.

Do you help with the launch itself?

Yes. After the memo we can help through the first 90 days: bringing on supply, the first orders and the weekly numbers.

We're already operating here. Is this for us?

Probably not the entry memo. A pricing review or an incentive audit is likely more useful. Get in touch and we'll work out which.

What do you need from us?

Your business model, any numbers from your other markets, and one person who can make the decision.
